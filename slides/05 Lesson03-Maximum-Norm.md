First an unfamiliar example

Consider the vector space $C[a,b]$ of all **real** continuous functions on the interval $[a,b]$.

**First of all can this be made into a vector space?**

Yes, by the usual definition of pointwise addition and scaling over the 
field $\mathbb{R}$.

So we have a vector space

We now define **a norm**

<h3 class="h3-blue">Definition:Maximum norm</h3>

$\forall \mathbf{f} \in C[a,b], \lVert \mathbf{f} \rVert= \max_{x \in [a,b]} \lvert f(x)\rvert$


Is this a valid definition at all? 

Yes it is - all continuous functions on a closed interval assumes their maximum in that interval.
