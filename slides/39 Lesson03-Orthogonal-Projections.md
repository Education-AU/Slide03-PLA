There are four important points here

1. We can find **the** vector parallel to $\mathbf{v}$ that is closest to $\mathbf{u}$
   in the $L_2$-norm.

2. From **any** $\mathbf{u}$, we can construct a vector orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$
   such that $\mathbf{u}$ can be expressed as the sum of a vector parallel to
   $\mathbf{v}$ and a vector orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$.

3. These two vectors constitute an orthogonal basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$.

4. **Gram–Schmidt**  
   This construction makes it possible to construct an orthogonal basis from
   any basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$. (PLA, p. 149)
