The norm structure provides the concept of length and distance. But it does not provide the concept of angle. To obtain
a notion of angle we define the concept of an inner product.

The inner product is defined slightly different on a real and a complex vector space

<h3 class="h3-blue">Definition: Real vector space inner product</h3>


1. The map<br>
$\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle: V\times V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
2. Symmetry<br>
$\langle \mathbf{u},\mathbf{v} \rangle=\langle \mathbf{v},\mathbf{u} \rangle$
3. Linearity<br>
$\langle s \mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w} \rangle=s \langle \mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\rangle+\langle \mathbf{v},\mathbf{w} \rangle$
4. Positive definite<br>
$\langle \mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle\geq 0 , \langle \mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle= 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}$
